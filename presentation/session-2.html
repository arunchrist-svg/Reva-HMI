QUESTIONAGAIN

What is user research?

DEFINITIONAFTER CASES

User research

A systematic way to gather evidence about people’s goals, tasks, and behaviour in context — so design decisions are based on reality, not assumption.

FRAMETWO AXES

Two useful axes.

Attitudinal

  • What people say, believe, prefer

Behavioural

  • What people do, attempt, abandon

Great research often combines both — but knows which one it is collecting.

FRAMESCALE

Qualitative vs quantitative.

Qualitative

  • Why and how
  • Depth, stories, context

Quantitative

  • How many and how often
  • Scale, comparison, trends
METHODCORE THREE

Three methods for this studio.

  1. 01Interview — past behaviour and language.
  2. 02Observation — what happens without your tour.
  3. 03Usability test — can they complete a task?
METHODQUESTION QUALITY

From opinion to behaviour.

Weak

  • Do you like this?
  • Would you use this?
  • Do you want more pockets?

Stronger

  • Walk me through the last time you paid.
  • What did your hands do first?
  • Where did you slow down?

DEBRIEFQUALITY

Where did the interview get thin?

Leading. Explaining. Filling silence. Jumping to solutions.

CHEAT SHEETINTERVIEW

Interview cheat sheet.

  1. 01Past, not future. Last real time only.
  2. 02One question. Ask, then stop.
  3. 03Follow verbs. Paid, searched, hid, dropped, waited.
  4. 04Write quotes. Their words beat your summary.
CHEAT SHEETPROBES

When stuck, probe:

  • Tell me more about that.
  • What happened just before?
  • What did you do next?
  • Can you show me with your hands?

METHODSLIBRARY

Which method should you use?

Choose by the research question — then by time, access, and risk.

METHODSDECISION RULE

Decision rule.

  • Why / meaning? Interview or contextual inquiry.
  • What happens? Observation or analytics.
  • Does it work? Usability test.
  • How to organise? Card sort or tree test.
  • Over days? Diary study.
METHODINTERVIEW

Interview

Best for motives, language, context, and past behaviour.

  1. 01When: early discovery; unclear problem.
  2. 02Risk: polite answers; imagined futures.
  3. 03Evidence: quotes, tasks, constraints, fears.
CASE STUDYINTERVIEW

Case: IDEO shopping cart · ABC Nightline 1999.

Team interviewed shoppers, store staff, cart makers, and repair experts.

  1. 01Ask. Repair expert: plastic carts catch wind; clocked at 35 mph in parking lots.
  2. 02Find. ~22,000 child hospitalisations/year; cart theft ~$800M industry cost.
  3. 03Learn. Expert interviews surfaced risks designers would not invent in a studio.
METHODOBSERVATION

Observation

Best when actions matter more than explanations.

  1. 01When: queues, counters, public tasks.
  2. 02Risk: you interpret too fast; people notice you.
  3. 03Evidence: steps, pauses, workarounds, errors.
CASE STUDYOBSERVATION

Case: IDEO grocery-store field day.

Same Nightline project — ~2 hours watching real shoppers in stores.

  1. 01Watch. Most shoppers never let go of the cart; pros leave it and roam.
  2. 02Find. Four need areas: child safety · theft · shopping process · finding items.
  3. 03Learn. Watching behaviour beat asking “What cart do you want?”
METHODCONTEXTUAL INQUIRY

Contextual inquiry

Observe the task, then ask in the same place.

  1. 01When: body, tools, place, and timing matter.
  2. 02Risk: interrupting the natural flow.
  3. 03Evidence: journey steps + reasons.
CASE STUDYCONTEXTUAL INQUIRY

Case: anaesthesia work · London hospital.

Published CI study (Cognition, Technology & Work) using Beyer & Holtzblatt.

  1. 01Work. 11 contextual inquiries · ~40 hours · 11 operations.
  2. 02Find. Infusion-pump visibility gaps; handovers lost critical info.
  3. 03Learn. Asking at the bedside revealed coordination lab interviews miss.
METHODUSABILITY TEST

Usability test

Best when you have a prototype and a clear task.

  1. 01When: validate folds, labels, pay flow.
  2. 02Rule: do not teach; observe the attempt.
  3. 03Evidence: success, time, errors, severity.
CASE STUDYUSABILITY TEST

Case: Sun Microsystems website · Nielsen 1995.

Task-based tests of homepage icons and old button bar — published on NN/g.

  1. 01Task. Guess icons with no labels; then use the live site for set tasks.
  2. 02Find. “Under construction” worker icon hated; lightning looked destructive.
  3. 03Learn. Making header buttons look clickable → +416% use (vs +48% site traffic).
METHODSAMPLE

How many people?

Nielsen & Landauer model · NN/g — qualitative usability, not surveys.

  1. 01Usability: ~5 users often find ~85% of issues in one design — then fix & retest.
  2. 02Interview: stop when the same patterns keep repeating.
  3. 03Survey: need larger N — only after you know what to measure.
METHODSURVEY

Survey

Best for scale after the problem is already clear.

Good for

  • How many? How often?

Weak for

  • Discovering unknown behaviour
CASE STUDYSURVEY

Case: Net Promoter Score · Reichheld / Bain.

Harvard Business Review 2003 — one question adopted across industries.

  1. 01Ask. “How likely are you to recommend us to a friend?” (0–10).
  2. 02Find. % promoters (9–10) minus % detractors (0–6) = NPS.
  3. 03Learn. Scales loyalty trends — does not invent why people struggle.
METHODCARD SORT

Card sort / tree test

Best when grouping and findability are the problem.

  1. 01Card sort: how people group items.
  2. 02Tree test: can they find the right place?
  3. 03Evidence: labels, categories, missed paths.
CASE STUDYCARD SORT

Case: Eurostar website IA · ~2005.

Open online card sort to rebuild navigation across UK / Europe / global.

  1. 01Work. ~80 cards · 180 travellers · 3 countries · 11,000+ pages to simplify.
  2. 02Find. Users grouped chronologically + booking / trip-planning — not org chart.
  3. 03Learn. Closed sort later validated 4 of 5 top-level groups.
METHODDIARY STUDY

Diary study

Best when behaviour unfolds across days.

  1. 01When: habits, routines, repeated friction.
  2. 02Risk: incomplete entries; memory bias.
  3. 03Evidence: patterns over time.
CASE STUDYDIARY STUDY

Case: Coursera learner diary · Liu 2022.

First diary study at Coursera — how learners use a new material type.

  1. 01Work. 14 days · 12 learners · 10 daily prompts (5 in-situ + 5 end-of-day).
  2. 02Find. Triggers and blockers in real life — richer than one-off interviews.
  3. 03Learn. Habits across days cannot be seen in a single lab session.
METHODSYNTHESIS

Affinity synthesis

Best after research, when notes feel messy.

  1. 01One note per quote or observation.
  2. 02Cluster by shared behaviour.
  3. 03Name the pattern as an insight with evidence.
CASE STUDYSYNTHESIS

Case: IDEO affinity wall · Nightline day 1.

Teams returned from the field and posted observations on the walls.

  1. 01Notes. Share everything learned — vote with Post-its, not opinions.
  2. 02Clusters. Four themes: child safety · theft · shopping process · finding items.
  3. 03Learn. Split into theme teams — prototypes follow clusters, not lone ideas.
QUALITYBIAS

Protect the evidence.

  • Do not lead the witness.
  • Do not sell your design during research.
  • Do not treat one loud person as “all users.”
  • Consent, privacy, and respect for embarrassment.
